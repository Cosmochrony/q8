  \title{Toward a Full-Rank Effective Metric:\\
  No Central Coefficient from the Heisenberg Commutator, and Scale-Free Casimir Rigidity\\[0.4em]
  \large Q8 of the Cosmochrony Spectral Geometry Programme}
  \author{J\'er\^ome Beau\\
  \small Independent Researcher\\
  \small \texttt{jerome.beau@cosmochrony.org}}
  \date{2026}

  \begin{abstract}
    The effective operator of Q5b, conditional on its spatial limit and lifting hypotheses, has a spatial principal
    symbol of rank two with an empty central slot; completing it to a four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric requires
    a positive coefficient $A_Z$ of $k_Z^2$ (open problem Q5b-O2).
    We prove that the Heisenberg commutator $[X, Y] = Z$ does not supply it.
    In exponential coordinates the sub-Laplacian $X^2 + Y^2$ has no first-order part, the principal symbol of its
    negative is a sum of two squares of rank two at every point, and its $k_Z^2$ coefficient is position-dependent
    and vanishes at the
    identity; no term of lower differential order changes this.
    Under the Carnot dilations the sub-Laplacian is homogeneous of degree two while $Z^2$ is homogeneous of degree
    four, so a constant central coefficient requires a new operator, and the ratio of central to horizontal
    coefficients is not dilation-invariant: equality of the two selects a length scale.
    If a positive rank-three spatial form is supplied together with the compatibility conditions of Q7 and is
    invariant under the full $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ action, Schur's lemma gives $A_H = A_Z = 2\lambda$ with $\lambda > 0$
    free; rotation invariance about one axis does not suffice.
    The Casimir acts as 2 on the spin-one module, but this fixes the normalisation of one invariant form, not a
    coefficient.
    No value of $A_Z$ is derived, the passage from the rank-two symbol to a positive rank-three form is not made, and
    Q5b-O2 remains open.
    Interpretive outlook: the central direction of the emergent space is not generated by the non-commutativity of
    its horizontal directions at the level of the metric, and isotropy between the two kinds of direction is a
    statement about a physical length scale, which the substrate would have to supply.
  \end{abstract}

  \textbf{Keywords:} Heisenberg group; sub-Laplacian; principal symbol; Carnot dilations; su(2)-invariant forms;
  Casimir operator; Schur's lemma; effective metric; emergent geometry; Cosmochrony.

  \section{Introduction}

    \subsection{The central slot of the effective co-metric}

      Paper Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} extracts, conditionally on its spatial limit hypothesis [H-L] and on the lifting
      hypothesis [H-lift], an effective operator $\Leff$ on $\mathbb{R}_{\tau} \times \heisR$ whose spatial part is
      $\AH\,\Delta_H$, with $\Delta_H = X^2 + Y^2$ the sub-Laplacian, and a leading-order co-metric
      $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, \AH, \AH, 0)$ (Q5b Theorem~5.2).
      Open problem Q5b-O2 asks for a full-rank extension, which would require a positive coefficient $\Az$ of $k_Z^2$.
      Paper Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} does not discharge [H-lift] and establishes no bridge non-obstruction.
      Paper Q7~\cite{Beau2026q7} states what an equivariant identification between the admissible spin-one module
      $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ and a three-dimensional spatial sector would require, and shows that Q5b's rank-two symbol cannot
      be congruent to a positive rank-three form.

      This paper answers two questions.
      Can the Heisenberg commutator $[X, Y] = Z$ generate the missing central coefficient?
      And if a positive rank-three spatial form is supplied, what does $\suTwo$ representation theory fix about its
      coefficients?

    \subsection{Main result}

      The input [T] and the invariance condition [INV$^+$] are stated in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

      \begin{theorem}[Central direction: rank obstruction and scale-free rigidity]\label{thm:main}
        The following hold.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item[(i)] The sub-Laplacian $\Delta_H$ of $\heisR$ has, in exponential coordinates, no first-order part,
          and the principal symbol of $-\Delta_H$ is $(k_X + \frac{y}{2}k_Z)^2 + (k_Y - \frac{x}{2}k_Z)^2$, of rank
          two at every point.
          No operator obtained from $\AH\,\Delta_H$ by adding terms of lower differential order has a principal
          symbol of rank three.
          Under the Carnot dilations, $\Delta_H$ is homogeneous of degree two and a term $Z^2$ is homogeneous of
          degree four.
          \item[(ii)] Assume \textup{[T]} and \textup{[INV$^+$]}.
          Then the transported spatial form on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ is $\AH\,Q_\pm + \Az\,Q_0$ with
          \begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
            \AH = \Az = 2\lambda, \qquad \lambda > 0,
          \end{equation}
          so that the transported form is $\lambda$ times the Casimir form, and $\lambda$ is not determined by
          \textup{[T]}, \textup{[INV$^+$]} or $\suTwo$-invariance.
          \item[(iii)] Under \textup{[T]} with rotation invariance about a single axis in place of \textup{[INV$^+$]},
          no relation between $\AH$ and $\Az$ follows.
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{theorem}

      Item~(i) is unconditional; it shows that Q5b-O2 cannot be settled from $\Leff$ by any lower-order correction,
      and that a left-invariant operator with a positive constant central coefficient must contain a term $Z^2$ of
      homogeneous degree four.
      Items~(ii) and~(iii) concern such a new operator, when one is supplied.
      The form of~\eqref{eq:main} records the Casimir normalisation; it does not fix $\Az$.
      In particular $\Az = 2$ is not a consequence of anything proved here or cited.

    \subsection{Organisation}

      Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes conventions and states [T] and [INV$^+$].
      Section~\ref{sec:source} proves item~(i) in exponential coordinates.
      Section~\ref{sec:casimir} proves items~(ii) and~(iii).
      Section~\ref{sec:beals} proves the homogeneity statement and draws its consequence for scales.
      Section~\ref{sec:consequences} states the consequences for Q5b-O2, and Section~\ref{sec:summary} summarises.

  \section{Setup and Inputs}\label{sec:setup}

    \subsection{Coordinates on the Heisenberg group}

      We use exponential coordinates $(x, y, z)$ on $\heisR$ with the group law
      $(x, y, z)\cdot(x', y', z') = (x + x',\, y + y',\, z + z' + \tfrac12(x'y - xy'))$.
      The left-invariant horizontal fields and their commutator are
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fields}
        X = \partial_x + \tfrac{y}{2}\,\partial_z, \qquad
        Y = \partial_y - \tfrac{x}{2}\,\partial_z, \qquad
        Z := [X, Y] = -\partial_z .
      \end{equation}
      The field $Z$ is central.
      The Carnot dilations are $\delta_r(x, y, z) = (rx, ry, r^2 z)$ for $r > 0$; $X$ and $Y$ are homogeneous of
      degree one and $Z$ of degree two.
      The symbol of a differential operator is taken with $\partial_x \mapsto i k_X$, $\partial_y \mapsto i k_Y$ and
      $\partial_z \mapsto i k_Z$, so that the symbol of $\Delta_H$ reproduces the symbol of Q7 (Section~4.5)
      up to sign.
      A co-metric is read as the coefficient matrix of the second-order part, that is, as $-\sigmatwo$; a positive
      coefficient of $k_Z^2$ in a co-metric is a positive coefficient of $\partial_z^2 = Z^2$ in the operator.

    \subsection{The spin-one module and the supplied target}

      Let $\Vr \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ be the spin-$\frac12$ module of the supplied carrier of O23~\cite{Beau2026a27},
      and $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ the spin-one module with weight vectors $e_+, e_0, e_-$ of $J_3$-weights $+1, 0, -1$,
      orthonormal for an $\suTwo$-invariant Hermitian inner product $h_0$.
      Write $Q_\pm$ and $Q_0$ for the orthogonal projections onto $\spn\{e_+, e_-\}$ and $\spn\{e_0\}$.
      Q7 isolates four conditions: [ID], the identification of the admissible space $\heff$ with $\Sym^2(\Vr)$;
      [ACT], an adjoint $\suTwo$ action on the real spatial sector $\Wsp$; [INV], invariance of the real spatial form
      under the rotations generated by $J_3$, with compatibility with the real structure; and [ADAPT], an adapted
      equivariant isomorphism $\varphi_0$ sending the weight $\pm 1$ plane to the horizontal plane and the weight $0$
      line to the central line (Q7 Section~4 and Remark~4.6).
      None is established for a geometric construction.

      \begin{hypo}[{[T]}, supplied target]
        A candidate continuum operator $\Lext$ (Q7 Criterion~5.1) has a positive rank-three spatial form
        $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ on $\Wsp$, and [ACT], [INV] and [ADAPT] hold, so that the pullback of its Hermitian
        extension along the adapted isomorphism $\varphi_0$ is $\AH\,Q_\pm + \Az\,Q_0$ with $\AH, \Az > 0$
        (Q7 Lemma~4.5(3) with $\lambda = 1$).
      \end{hypo}

      Along $\lambda_0\varphi_0$ with $\lambda_0 \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ both coefficients are multiplied by
      $|\lambda_0|^2$; fixing $\varphi_0$ makes $\AH$ and $\Az$ the entries of $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ in the adapted frame.
      Hypothesis [ID] is not part of [T]: it is needed only to transfer statements from $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ to the measured
      space $\heff$ (Q7 Remark~4.6), and every statement below is made on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ and $\Wsp$.

      \begin{hypo}[{[INV$^+$]}, full invariance]
        The Hermitian extension of $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is invariant under the whole $\suTwo$ action of [ACT], not only
        under the rotations generated by $J_3$.
      \end{hypo}

      Condition [INV] of Q7 makes the two horizontal coefficients equal and removes cross terms with the central line;
      it does not force $\AH = \Az$ (Q7, proof of Lemma~4.5).
      Condition [INV$^+$] is strictly stronger, and no source supplies it.

  \section{The Commutator Does Not Generate a Central Coefficient}\label{sec:source}

    \subsection{The sub-Laplacian in exponential coordinates}

      \begin{proposition}[No central quadratic term from the commutator]\label{prop:source}
        With the fields~\eqref{eq:fields},
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:sublap}
          \Delta_H = X^2 + Y^2
          = \partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 + y\,\partial_x\partial_z - x\,\partial_y\partial_z
          + \tfrac{x^2 + y^2}{4}\,\partial_z^2 ,
        \end{equation}
        with no first-order and no zeroth-order part.
        The principal symbol of $-\Delta_H$ is
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol}
          p(x, y; k) = \Bigl(k_X + \tfrac{y}{2}\,k_Z\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(k_Y - \tfrac{x}{2}\,k_Z\Bigr)^2 ,
        \end{equation}
        a positive semi-definite quadratic form in $k$ of rank two at every point, with kernel spanned by
        $(-\frac{y}{2}, \frac{x}{2}, 1)$.
        Its $k_Z^2$ coefficient is $(x^2 + y^2)/4$, which vanishes at the identity.
        For every $\AH > 0$ and every differential operator $R$ of order at most one, the principal symbol of
        $\AH\,\Delta_H + R$ is $-\AH\,p$ and has rank two.
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        The coefficients of $X$ and $Y$ do not depend on $z$, and $\partial_x(y/2) = 0$, $\partial_y(x/2) = 0$, so
        $X^2 = \partial_x^2 + y\,\partial_x\partial_z + \frac{y^2}{4}\partial_z^2$ and
        $Y^2 = \partial_y^2 - x\,\partial_y\partial_z + \frac{x^2}{4}\partial_z^2$, which gives~\eqref{eq:sublap}.
        Replacing each derivative by $i$ times the corresponding dual variable in the second-order part gives
        $-p$ as the principal symbol of $\Delta_H$.
        The form $p$ is the sum of the squares of the two linear forms $k_X + \frac{y}{2}k_Z$ and
        $k_Y - \frac{x}{2}k_Z$, which are linearly independent at every point, so $p$ has rank two, and its kernel is
        their common zero set.
        The principal symbol of an operator of order two is determined by its second-order part, which $R$ does not
        change.
        The commutator relation enters only through the coefficients of $X$ and $Y$: indeed
        $[X, Y] = \partial_x(-\frac{x}{2})\partial_z - \partial_y(\frac{y}{2})\partial_z = -\partial_z$.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[What the commutator does contribute]\label{rem:commutator}
        The commutator is responsible for the position dependence of~\eqref{eq:symbol}, for the hypoellipticity of
        $\Delta_H$ by H\"ormander's bracket condition, and for the homogeneous dimension $D_{\mathrm{hom}} = 4$.
        It does not produce a constant positive co-metric coefficient of $k_Z^2$: a first-order term in $Z$ has a purely
        imaginary symbol linear in $k_Z$, and at the identity, where $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ reduce to $\partial_x$,
        $\partial_y$ and $-\partial_z$, the $k_Z^2$ coefficient of a left-invariant second-order operator is the
        coefficient of its $Z^2$ term.
        A central coefficient therefore has to come from a new operator, such as one supplying the extended target of
        Q7 Conjecture~4.7.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Invariant Forms and the Supplied Target}\label{sec:casimir}

    \subsection{What invariance fixes}

      \begin{lemma}[Invariant forms on the spin-one module]\label{lem:casimir-unique}
        Every $\suTwo$-invariant Hermitian form $h$ on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ is $h = a\,h_0$ with $a \in \mathbb{R}$, and $h$ is
        positive definite exactly when $a > 0$.
        For generators normalised by $[J_1, J_2] = iJ_3$ and cyclically, the Casimir
        $\Csu = J_1^2 + J_2^2 + J_3^2$ acts as $2 \cdot \mathrm{Id}$, so the Casimir form
        $Q_C(v) := h_0(v, \Csu v) = 2\,h_0(v, v)$ is the point $a = 2$ of this ray.
        Invariance does not determine $a$, and rescaling the generators by $\kappa$ multiplies the Casimir by
        $\kappa^2$.
      \end{lemma}

      \begin{proof}
        Write $h(v, w) = h_0(v, Tw)$ with $T$ self-adjoint for $h_0$.
        Invariance of $h$ and $h_0$ makes $T$ commute with the action; $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ is irreducible, so
        $T = a\,\mathrm{Id}$ by Schur's lemma, and self-adjointness makes $a$ real.
        The Casimir acts on the spin-$j$ module as $j(j+1)$, which is $2$ for $j = 1$, and the last assertion is
        immediate from the definition of $\Csu$.
      \end{proof}

    \subsection{Transport of a supplied invariant form}

      \begin{proposition}[Scale-free rigidity of a supplied form]\label{prop:bridge-casimir}
        Assume \textup{[T]}.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item[(a)] Under \textup{[INV$^+$]}, $\AH = \Az$, and writing the common value as $2\lambda$ with
          $\lambda > 0$ expresses the transported form as $\lambda\,Q_C$.
          The number $\lambda$ is not determined by \textup{[T]} and \textup{[INV$^+$]}.
          \item[(b)] Without \textup{[INV$^+$]}, for every $a, b > 0$ there is a target satisfying \textup{[T]} whose
          transported form is $a\,Q_\pm + b\,Q_0$.
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        (a) Under [ADAPT] the isomorphism $\varphi_0$ is equivariant, so the pullback of an invariant Hermitian form is
        invariant on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$.
        By Lemma~\ref{lem:casimir-unique} the pullback is $a\,h_0$ with $a > 0$, and comparison with
        $\AH\,Q_\pm + \Az\,Q_0$ gives $\AH = \Az = a$; put $\lambda = a/2$.
        Replacing $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ by $t\,g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with $t > 0$ preserves [T] and [INV$^+$] and multiplies
        $\lambda$ by $t$.
        (b) Take $\Wsp$ with the standard real spin-one action and $\varphi_0$ adapted, and let $\Lext$ have constant
        spatial coefficients, with $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ diagonal with entries $a, a, b$ in the corresponding real basis.
        It is invariant under the rotations generated by $J_3$, compatible with the real structure, positive of
        rank three, and its transport along $\varphi_0$ is $a\,Q_\pm + b\,Q_0$.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[The number 2 is a normalisation]
        The value $2$ in~\eqref{eq:main} is the Casimir eigenvalue in a chosen normalisation of the generators; it
        fixes which invariant form is called the Casimir form, not the size of a geometric coefficient.
        Fixing $\lambda$ by matching $\AH$ to a value obtained elsewhere is legitimate only when that value is obtained
        without reference to $\Az$; the conditional isotropy theorem of Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} takes the scale as an
        independent input, so the two papers together fix no value.
        A coincidence of a discrete compression entry with the number $2$ is not normalisation-invariant and carries
        no evidential weight (Q7 Remark~6.3).
      \end{remark}

  \section{Homogeneity and Scale}\label{sec:beals}

    \subsection{Homogeneous degree under the Carnot dilations}

      \begin{proposition}[Homogeneous degree two excludes $Z^2$]\label{prop:bg}
        The left-invariant differential operators on $\heisR$ that are homogeneous of degree two under the Carnot
        dilations form the span of $X^2$, $Y^2$, $XY + YX$ and $Z$.
        None of them has a nonzero constant $k_Z^2$ coefficient in its principal symbol.
        The operator $Z^2$ is homogeneous of degree four.
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        By the Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt theorem, the monomials $X^a Y^b Z^c$ form a basis of the left-invariant
        differential operators, and each is homogeneous of degree $a + b + 2c$.
        Degree two leaves $X^2$, $XY$, $Y^2$ and $Z$, and $XY = \frac12(XY + YX) + \frac12 Z$.
        By Proposition~\ref{prop:source} the principal symbols of $X^2$ and $Y^2$ have $k_Z^2$ coefficients $-y^2/4$
        and $-x^2/4$, and the same computation gives $xy/2$ for $XY + YX$; $Z$ is of first order.
        A combination of these therefore has a $k_Z^2$ coefficient that is a quadratic polynomial in $(x, y)$ without
        constant term, which is constant only if it vanishes identically.
        The degree of $Z^2$ is $2 \cdot 2$.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Isotropy selects a length scale]\label{rem:scale}
        Let $L_{A_H, A_Z} = A_H\,\Delta_H + A_Z\,Z^2$ with constants $A_H, A_Z > 0$.
        Since $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ are homogeneous of degrees one, one and two, the pullback of $L_{A_H, A_Z}$ by the
        dilation $\delta_r$ is $L_{r^2 A_H,\, r^4 A_Z}$.
        The ratio $A_Z/A_H$ is therefore multiplied by $r^2$ and is not dilation-invariant: the equality
        $A_H = A_Z$ holds at one scale only, and asserting it fixes a unit of length along the central direction.
        On the finite groups $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ such a unit could come from the discretisation,
        but its value then depends on how the continuum limit is taken.
        Any operator supplying [T] with $\AH = \Az$ thus carries a length scale that neither Q5b's operator nor
        representation theory provides.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Consequences for the Effective Metric and Q5b-O2}\label{sec:consequences}

    \subsection{Status of Q5b-O2}

      By Proposition~\ref{prop:source}, the co-metric of Q5b Theorem~5.2 cannot be completed to full rank by any
      lower-order correction of $\Leff$.
      A left-invariant completion whose spatial co-metric is positive definite at the identity needs a positive
      $k_Z^2$ coefficient there, which by the argument of Remark~\ref{rem:commutator} is the coefficient of a
      term $Z^2$, of
      homogeneous degree four (Proposition~\ref{prop:bg}); it carries a scale (Remark~\ref{rem:scale}).
      Q5b-O2 is open.

      \begin{corollary}[Conditional form of a full-rank co-metric]\label{cor:metric}
        Assume \textup{[T]} and \textup{[INV$^+$]}, and assume that $\Lext$ has temporal coefficient $-A_\tau$ with
        $A_\tau > 0$ and no mixed temporal--spatial terms in the adapted frame.
        Then its co-metric in that frame is $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda)$ with $\lambda > 0$,
        non-degenerate of rank four and of signature $(-,+,+,+)$.
        The value of $\lambda$ is not determined.
      \end{corollary}

      \begin{proof}
        By Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge-casimir}(a) the spatial block is $2\lambda$ times the identity in the adapted
        frame; the temporal block is the stated assumption.
      \end{proof}

      The Lorentzian signature of Q5b Theorem~6.1 concerns Q5b's own operator, conditional on [H-L], [H-lift] and a
      hyperbolicity hypothesis; it is not a statement about $\Lext$.

    \subsection{The bridge question}

      Q7 Conjecture~4.7, the existence of an extended target with the listed compatibility conditions, is open.
      The present results neither construct nor exclude it.
      They add two requirements to it: a positive central coefficient must come from a term of homogeneous degree
      four, and isotropy requires [INV$^+$] and a length scale.
      Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} states the further inputs under which fingerprint uniformity would force the ratio
      $\AH/\Az$ towards one; none of them is established.

    \subsection{What would fix a coefficient}

      Representation theory fixes the shape of an invariant form and leaves its size free
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:casimir-unique}); the Carnot structure makes the relative size of central and horizontal
      coefficients scale-dependent (Section~\ref{sec:beals}).
      A value of $\Az$, and in particular the value $2$, would therefore require a dimensionful datum of the
      construction, obtained independently of $\AH$.

  \section{Summary and Open Problems}\label{sec:summary}

    \subsection{What this paper establishes}

      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The Heisenberg commutator does not generate a central coefficient: the principal symbol of the
        sub-Laplacian has rank two everywhere, and lower-order terms do not change it
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:source}).
        \item Left-invariant operators of homogeneous degree two carry no constant $k_Z^2$ term, and the ratio of
        central to horizontal coefficients scales as $r^2$ under dilations (Proposition~\ref{prop:bg},
        Remark~\ref{rem:scale}).
        \item For a supplied positive rank-three target with full $\suTwo$-invariance, $\AH = \Az = 2\lambda$ with
        $\lambda > 0$ free; rotation invariance about one axis gives no relation (Lemma~\ref{lem:casimir-unique},
        Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge-casimir}).
        \item No value of $\Az$ is derived, and Q5b-O2 is open.
      \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{Remaining open problems}

      \textbf{Q8-O1} \emph{(Extended target).}
      Construct an operator or effective form supplying [T], with a central term of homogeneous degree four, and
      relate it to Q5b's horizontal geometry.

      \textbf{Q8-O2} \emph{(Full invariance).}
      Derive [INV$^+$] for such a construction, or show that it fails; without it no relation between $\AH$ and
      $\Az$ follows.

      \textbf{Q8-O3} \emph{(Scale).}
      Identify the length scale that a central term of degree four carries, and a value of $\Az$ obtained without
      reference to $\AH$.
      The temporal coefficient $A_\tau$ (Q5b-O3) is not addressed here.

    \subsection{Interpretive outlook}

      \emph{This subsection is interpretation, not a result.}
      In the emergent geometry of the programme, the central direction of the Heisenberg group is the one produced by
      the non-commutativity of the two horizontal directions.
      The results above show that this production happens at the level of reachability and dimension, not at the
      level of the metric: the commutator makes the central direction accessible, but it does not assign it a length.
      Giving the central direction a length comparable to the horizontal ones is an additional physical input, and
      because the two kinds of direction scale differently, that input is a length scale.
      For a reader situating the programme, the question of a full-rank isotropic metric is therefore a question
      about which feature of the substrate sets a unit of length, not a question that the symmetry group settles on
      its own.
